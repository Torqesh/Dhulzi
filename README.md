# Dhulzi

> The library lends a mirror to the desert and receives a tongue of snow.

## The Shelves Beyond the Salt Road

The library stands where the salt road becomes too white to follow. Its outer walls contain no entrance. Readers reach the interior by sleeping beside the wall with a closed book beneath one hand.

Inside, the shelves hold mirrors instead of volumes. Their frames are labeled with titles that resemble instructions for weather: *When the West Turns Quiet*, *A Noon Beneath the Well*, *The Rain That Arrived Without Water*.

The librarian wears a linen covering across her mouth. She writes each loan on a strip of thin wood and breaks the strip in two. The reader keeps one half. The other is buried in a pot of dark sand beside her desk.

No mirror may be returned covered. No title may be spoken while its reflection contains a person who has not yet entered the room.

## The Reader With a Cracked Compass

A reader arrived carrying a compass whose glass had broken without leaving an opening. A narrow crack crossed its face, and behind that crack moved a little white cloud.

He wished to borrow a mirror showing the place where the compass had stopped pointing north. The librarian selected a narrow frame from the lowest shelf. Its title was *A Shore Before the First Footprint*.

In the glass, the reader saw a desert with a line of dark tents. Beside the nearest tent stood a person holding the compass before its glass had cracked. Their other hand rested on a small bowl filled with snow.

The reader recognized the tents but not the snow. He had crossed that desert during a season when even the shadows of stones were warm.

The librarian broke the wooden loan strip. The compass needle turned toward its buried half.

## The Loan That Traveled Alone

He carried the mirror outside. The salt road appeared within it, while the actual ground beneath his feet became dark sand. Behind him, the library's wall resembled the side of a vast closed book.

By evening, the mirror had grown cold. Its desert tents became small windows, each opening onto the same library shelf. The person with the snow bowl moved between them, leaving footprints that vanished before their second step.

The reader placed the compass against the frame. The white cloud behind its crack lengthened into a tongue of snow. It touched the glass and left a single clear patch through which he could see a staircase descending beneath the dunes.

He set the mirror on the ground. Its reflection remained upright. Beneath that standing reflection, the staircase acquired a handrail made of pale horn.

## The Archive Below the Dunes

At the foot of the stairs lay a dragon curled around a chamber of empty frames. Its scales were polished enough to show the library from different centuries. Its folded wing supported the salt road above.

The snow bowl rested beneath its tongue. The person from the tents stood beside it, holding a compass-shaped space in one hand. When the reader approached, the space filled with the weight of his own instrument.

The dragon had once swallowed the desert's first reflection. Since then, the library had been lending its mirrors outward, hoping one would return with the shape of what the sleeper could no longer name.

The librarian had covered her mouth because she had accidentally spoken that shape as a title. Every winter afterward, one mirror began showing a room with nobody inside it, and one reader failed to find the wall on waking.

## Words Along the Broken Strip

The reader's half of the loan strip carried four marks:

*Dhul — zi — em — qath.*

He held them toward the compass-shaped space. The buried half rose through the chamber floor, wrapped in roots of dark sand. Together, the two strips formed a word that could be seen only in reflection.

The person beside the dragon turned the snow bowl over. No snow fell. Instead, a narrow road of ordinary earth extended from its rim toward the stairs.

The reader placed his compass on that road. The crack became an opening at last, and the tongue of snow slipped through it. Across the chamber, every empty frame briefly showed the desert under a sky full of slow white clouds.

The dragon closed its mouth. The compass needle stopped pointing at anything within the room.

## A Return Without Its Cover

The reader woke beside the library wall with a closed book beneath his hand. Between its pages lay a thin wooden strip, whole and unmarked. His compass rested nearby, its glass clear, its needle turning normally toward the north.

He could find no mirror to return. Yet when he opened the book, its first page reflected the wall behind him, and in that reflection stood a narrow doorway.

He entered carrying the book uncovered. The librarian took it without writing a new account. Her linen mouth covering lay folded beside the pot of sand.

On the lowest shelf stood a mirror titled *The Desert After It Has Been Seen*.

The salt road remains difficult to follow. The outer wall remains without an entrance. But somewhere beneath it, a pale tongue holds a single snowflake that has forgotten to melt, and the empty frames no longer mistake silence for a missing face.
